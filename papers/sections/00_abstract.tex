\begin{abstract}
Professional traders routinely reason over candlestick charts, identifying trendlines, support/resistance levels, and chart formations before making decisions. Despite the centrality of visual chart analysis in financial practice, existing AI systems for financial decision-making either rely on raw numerical time-series encoders that discard spatial-relational structure, or naively feed chart screenshots to vision-language models without structured geometric understanding. We introduce \textsc{FinVL-MAS}, a multi-agent system that operationalizes the cognitive workflow of expert traders by: (1)~rendering financial data as structured chart visualizations, (2)~extracting chart geometry---trendlines, support/resistance zones, candlestick formations, and volume patterns---via a hybrid VLM and rule-based pipeline, and (3)~routing these visual representations through five specialized reasoning agents (Chart Analyst, Pattern Reasoner, Event Analyst, Risk Controller, and Decision Manager) coordinated via gated shared memory. We evaluate \textsc{FinVL-MAS} on CSI300 and S\&P500 daily trading tasks under realistic backtesting conditions with transaction costs, temporal splits, and point-in-time constraints. Our experiments demonstrate that structured visual reasoning captures decision-relevant patterns missed by numerical baselines, with the largest gains observed during volatile and pattern-rich market regimes. Ablation studies confirm the independent value of chart geometry extraction, multi-agent decomposition, and gated communication.
\end{abstract}
