\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview and Problem Formulation}

Given a target asset with historical OHLCV (open, high, low, close, volume) data up to time $t$, \textsc{FinVL-MAS} produces a trading decision $d_t \in \{\texttt{buy}, \texttt{sell}, \texttt{hold}\}$ with position size, stop-loss levels, and an interpretable rationale. The system processes data through a pipeline that mirrors the stages of expert trader cognition: perception, pattern recognition, contextual integration, risk assessment, and decision formation (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}).

Formally, let $\mathbf{X}_{t-L:t} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times 5}$ denote the lookback window of $L$ OHLCV bars. The pipeline produces:
\begin{equation}
    d_t = \text{DecisionPM}\Big(\mathcal{M}_t\Big), \quad \mathcal{M}_t = \bigoplus_{a \in \mathcal{A}} \text{GatedCom}\big(\mathcal{M}, h_a(\mathbf{X}_{t-L:t})\big)
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{A} = \{\text{ChartAnalyst}, \text{PatternReasoner}, \text{EventAnalyst}, \text{RiskController}\}$ are specialist agents, $\mathcal{M}$ is the shared memory, $h_a(\cdot)$ denotes agent $a$'s output representation, and $\text{GatedCom}$ is the gated cross-attention communication mechanism adapted from MAS4TS~\cite{mas4ts2025}. The full pipeline is summarised in Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{FinVL-MAS Decision Pipeline}
\label{alg:pipeline}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE OHLCV window $\mathbf{X}_{t-L:t}$, optional chart image $I_t$, optional event context $E_t$
\ENSURE Trading decision $d_t$ with rationale
\STATE $\mathcal{M} \leftarrow \emptyset$ \hfill \COMMENT{Initialise shared memory}
\STATE $I_t \leftarrow \text{Render}(\mathbf{X}_{t-L:t})$ \hfill \COMMENT{Candlestick chart with overlays}
\STATE $\mathcal{G}_t^{\text{rule}} \leftarrow \text{ExtractGeometry}(\mathbf{X}_{t-L:t})$ \hfill \COMMENT{Rule-based geometry}
\IF{VLM available}
    \STATE $\mathcal{G}_t^{\text{vlm}} \leftarrow \text{VLMAnalyze}(I_t, \mathcal{G}_t^{\text{rule}})$
    \STATE $\mathcal{G}_t \leftarrow \text{Merge}(\mathcal{G}_t^{\text{rule}}, \mathcal{G}_t^{\text{vlm}})$
\ELSE
    \STATE $\mathcal{G}_t \leftarrow \mathcal{G}_t^{\text{rule}}$
\ENDIF
\STATE $\mathcal{M} \leftarrow \text{GatedWrite}(\mathcal{M}, \text{ChartAnalyst}(\mathcal{G}_t))$
\STATE $\mathcal{M} \leftarrow \text{GatedWrite}(\mathcal{M}, \text{PatternReasoner}(\mathcal{M}))$
\STATE $\mathcal{M} \leftarrow \text{GatedWrite}(\mathcal{M}, \text{EventAnalyst}(E_t, \mathcal{M}))$
\STATE $\mathcal{M} \leftarrow \text{GatedWrite}(\mathcal{M}, \text{RiskController}(\mathbf{X}_{t-L:t}, \mathcal{M}))$
\STATE $d_t \leftarrow \text{DecisionPM}(\mathcal{M})$ \hfill \COMMENT{Confidence-weighted fusion}
\RETURN $d_t$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Visual Chart Analysis}
\label{sec:visual}

\paragraph{Chart rendering.}
The system renders $\mathbf{X}_{t-L:t}$ as a standardised candlestick chart with volume bars and configurable technical indicator overlays (moving averages, Bollinger bands). Rendering uses \texttt{mplfinance} with consistent axis scaling, colour coding, and resolution ($1024 \times 768$ at 150 DPI) to ensure visual consistency across assets and time periods.

\paragraph{Hybrid chart geometry extraction.}
We extract structured chart geometry $\mathcal{G}_t$ via a hybrid pipeline that combines rule-based precision with VLM-based holistic interpretation. The rule-based path applies well-defined algorithms: trendline detection via linear regression on local extrema (retaining fits with $R^2 > 0.3$), support and resistance identification through adaptive price clustering (tolerance scaled to the price range), candlestick pattern classification based on body-to-shadow ratios, and volume signal detection including climax and divergence patterns. The VLM path sends the rendered chart image to a vision-language model (GPT-4o) with structured prompts that request formation identification (double top/bottom, triangles, head-and-shoulders), regime assessment, and overall directional bias. The rule-based channel provides numerically precise geometric features, while the VLM captures complex spatial patterns that resist simple algorithmic description. Disagreements between the two channels are surfaced as uncertainty signals to downstream agents.

The complete geometry is represented as a typed structure:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{G}_t = \big(\{\ell_i\}, \{s_j\}, \{c_k\}, \{f_m\}, \{v_n\}, r_t\big)
\end{equation}
where $\ell_i$ are trendlines (direction, slope, $R^2$, breakout status), $s_j$ are support and resistance levels (price, strength, touch count), $c_k$ are candlestick patterns (type, significance, implication), $f_m$ are chart formations (type, stage, target price), $v_n$ are volume signals (type, implication), and $r_t$ is the classified market regime (trending, volatile, ranging, quiet).

\subsection{Multi-Agent Reasoning Architecture}
\label{sec:agents}

We decompose the trader decision process into five specialist agents, each corresponding to a recognisable stage of expert reasoning. The \textbf{ChartAnalystAgent} perceives chart geometry $\mathcal{G}_t$ and produces a visual summary with directional bias and confidence. The \textbf{PatternReasonerAgent} interprets detected patterns using historical base rates drawn from technical analysis literature~\cite{bulkowski2005encyclopedia} and regime context to form a directional hypothesis with associated probability estimates. The \textbf{EventAnalystAgent} integrates non-visual context (news sentiment, fundamental data) when available and provides a neutral assessment otherwise, allowing the system to operate with or without textual data. The \textbf{RiskControllerAgent} assesses downside risk by computing stop-loss levels from ATR(14), adjusting for nearby support zones, and determining position size based on volatility regime and risk classification. The \textbf{DecisionPMAgent} integrates all upstream evidence via confidence-weighted scoring, applies risk filtering, detects and reports inter-agent contradictions, and produces the final trading decision.

Agents execute sequentially (ChartAnalyst $\rightarrow$ PatternReasoner $\rightarrow$ EventAnalyst $\rightarrow$ RiskController $\rightarrow$ DecisionPM), with each agent reading the current shared memory snapshot and writing its outputs back with associated confidence scores.

\subsection{Shared Memory and Gated Communication}
\label{sec:memory}

Agents communicate through a shared memory $\mathcal{M}$ that supports confidence-weighted writes. When agent $a$ writes to memory, the update proceeds in two stages. First, a gated cross-attention mechanism adapted from MAS4TS~\cite{mas4ts2025} computes the candidate update:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathcal{M}} = \mathcal{M} + \sigma\big(\text{Gate}(h_a)\big) \odot \text{Attn}(Q{=}\mathcal{M},\; K{=}h_a,\; V{=}h_a)
\end{equation}
where $\sigma(\cdot)$ is a sigmoid gate and $h_a$ is the writing agent's hidden state. The gate learns to suppress noisy or irrelevant contributions. Second, the actual memory state is updated via confidence-weighted interpolation:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{M} \leftarrow (1 - \alpha_a) \cdot \mathcal{M}_{\text{prev}} + \alpha_a \cdot \hat{\mathcal{M}}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_a \in [0,1]$ is agent $a$'s self-assessed confidence. High-confidence agents exert greater influence on the shared state. The memory system additionally tracks the history of each key and performs automatic disagreement detection when bias-related entries (e.g., chart bullish vs.\ pattern bearish) conflict across agents. These contradictions are explicitly surfaced to the DecisionPM agent as uncertainty signals.

\subsection{Iterative Refinement Loop}
\label{sec:loop}

Inspired by R\&D-Agent~\cite{rdagent2025}, the system supports a closed-loop iterative workflow that progressively improves decision quality across episodes:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Hypothesis} \xrightarrow{\text{configure}} \text{Experiment} \xrightarrow{\text{backtest}} \text{Feedback} \xrightarrow{\text{reflect}} \text{Trace}
\end{equation}
Each iteration begins with hypothesis generation, where the system formulates testable claims about which visual patterns are most predictive under specific market conditions (e.g., trendline breaks during volatile regimes, or volume divergence preceding reversals). The hypothesis is operationalised as a configuration adjustment (modifying geometry extraction parameters, agent weights, or pattern confidence thresholds), and validated through a walk-forward backtest. The resulting performance metrics are compared against the previous iteration, and a reflection step analyses which changes contributed to improvement or degradation. Accumulated insights are recorded in a persistent trace that informs future hypothesis generation. The loop terminates after a fixed budget of $K$ iterations (default $K{=}5$) or when the improvement in Sharpe ratio between consecutive iterations falls below a threshold $\epsilon{=}0.01$.



\subsection{Visual Encoder SFT with Frozen Text and Time-Series Encoders}
\label{sec:visual_sft}

To align visual evidence with heterogeneous financial context while preserving stable semantics from non-visual modalities, we adopt a constrained SFT strategy. For each decision date, we build aligned tuples consisting of a lookback OHLCV window, point-in-time filing text, and a rendered candlestick image with local evidence regions. The text encoder and the time-series encoder remain frozen during SFT. Only the visual encoder is trainable. This design isolates visual adaptation and reduces overfitting risk under limited financial supervision.

Given a sample $x_t$, the frozen text and time-series encoders produce a fixed target representation $z_t^{\text{text-ts}}$. The trainable visual encoder maps the chart image to $z_t^{\text{vis}}$. We optimize a multi-task objective:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \lambda_{\text{dir}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{dir}} + \lambda_{\text{align}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}} + \lambda_{\text{factor}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{factor}},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{dir}}$ is direction classification loss, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}$ enforces representation consistency between $z_t^{\text{vis}}$ and $z_t^{\text{text-ts}}$, and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{factor}}$ supervises local bbox-factor predictions.

For local supervision, each rendered chart includes bounding boxes associated with quantitative factors such as trend strength, volatility clustering, momentum, and volume spike. This step injects geometric priors into visual adaptation and encourages the model to focus on financially relevant local structures instead of generic chart texture.

\subsection{Computational Considerations}
\label{sec:compute}

The per-decision computational cost is dominated by the VLM call, which requires one API invocation per decision point. Rule-based geometry extraction, agent reasoning, and backtesting are computationally inexpensive (collectively under 50ms per decision on a single CPU core). The system is designed to degrade gracefully when the VLM is unavailable: the rule-based geometry path alone provides a structured, if less holistic, chart representation. In our experiments, the VLM backend processes one chart in approximately 3--5 seconds, making the system practical for end-of-day trading decisions but not for intraday high-frequency applications.
