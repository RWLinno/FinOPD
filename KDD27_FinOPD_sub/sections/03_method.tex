\subsection{Framework Overview}

\textsc{FinOPD} separates what evolves from when it may evolve. Alpha-Factor Evolution first compiles and freezes a versioned library of executable expressions. In each OPD round, the current student produces trajectories from point-in-time information. Only after an outcome horizon closes may the frozen teacher inspect an estimated hindsight summary, the student and router update, and a finalized episode enter long-term memory. This schedule pairs a parametric policy with auditable factors and local experience.

Four deterministic specialists transform point-in-time OHLCV, events, routed factors, and risk constraints into typed evidence. A Qwen3.5-9B policy is the only token-generating deployable decision model; it converts that evidence and eligible episodic retrievals into a bounded JSON action. Cross-time episodic memory is updated separately from matured records.

\subsection{Financial Multi-Agent Decision Backbone}

\paragraph{Structured point-in-time evidence.}
\label{sec:point_in_time_evidence}
The reproducible path extracts \texttt{ChartGeometry} deterministically from the trailing OHLCV window: trend lines, support/resistance, formations, candle patterns, volume cues, and a four-way regime. Pattern, event, and risk modules add typed evidence and constraints. These modules do not generate trainable language tokens and do not provide embeddings to routing or retrieval. The final policy is a rank-16 LoRA adapter over Qwen3.5-9B~\cite{qwen35_2026}; optional chart-VLM utilities are outside the reported path.

\paragraph{Typed coordination and final policy.}
Four evidence specialists form the upstream chain: Chart Analyst, Pattern Reasoner, Event Analyst, and Risk Controller. Each specialist $i\in\{1,\ldots,4\}$ writes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:agent_message}
m_t^i=(e_t^i,c_t^i,a_t^i,\zeta_t^i),
\end{equation}
where $e_t^i$ is attributed evidence, $c_t^i\in[0,1]$ is confidence, $a_t^i$ is a proposed action or constraint, and $\zeta_t^i$ records uncertainty. A deterministic specialist manager first produces a fallback proposal $d_t^{\mathrm{spec}}$. The deployable token policy then maps its serialized trace, a compact market snapshot, routed factors $F_t$, and eligible episodes $E_t$ to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:decision_manager}
\mathbf w_t=\pi_{\theta}\!\left(o_t,d_t^{\mathrm{spec}},
\{m_t^i\}_{i=1}^{4},F_t,E_t\right),
\end{equation}
together with a confidence, conviction, position size, evidence list, risks, and rationale. The parser restricts action to \{buy, sell, hold\}, clips position size to $[0,0.30]$, forces zero exposure for hold, and falls back to $d_t^{\mathrm{spec}}$ on invalid JSON. Decision-local shared memory is distinct from the cross-time memory in Section~\ref{sec:belief}.

\subsection{Factor Evolution and Conditional Routing}
\label{sec:router}

\paragraph{Frozen factor library.}
Agentic factor miners increasingly combine constrained expressions with reusable experience and deterministic evaluation~\cite{alphaagent2025,factorminer2026,hubble2026,alphaforgebench2026}. Our pre-OPD research process generates and mutates expressions in a constrained DSL. Retained candidates must parse, use point-in-time fields, return finite non-constant values, and pass development evaluation. Cost-aware Information Ratio ranks the accepted records, which retain validity and redundancy metadata. Their ordered IDs, expressions, metadata, and content hash define the 157-record manifest $\mathcal F^\star=\{f_j\}_{j=1}^{M}$, $M=157$. Frozen before OPD, it permits selection but no factor mutation.

\paragraph{Factor-state top-$k$ routing.}
At date $t$, all 157 expressions are evaluated using only the trailing OHLCV window. Non-finite values are set to zero; the cross-sectional vector is centered by its median, divided by $1.4826$ times its median absolute deviation, clipped to $[-5,5]$, and zero-padded to $\mathbf x_t\in\mathbb R^{768}$. Let $\mathbf r_t$ be the four-way regime one-hot. A two-layer MLP maps $[\mathbf x_t;\mathbf r_t]$ to $M$ logits. Router pretraining uses repeated straight-through Gumbel--Softmax without replacement:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gumbel}
\begin{aligned}
\boldsymbol\ell_t&=R_{\psi}([\mathbf x_t;\mathbf r_t])\in\mathbb R^M,
&\gamma_{t,j}&\sim\operatorname{Gumbel}(0,1),\\
\mathbf z_t&=\operatorname{STGumbelTopK}_k(
\boldsymbol\ell_t,\tau),
&\mathcal I_t&=\{j:z_{t,j}=1\},\\
F_t&=\{f_j\in\mathcal F^\star:j\in\mathcal I_t\},
&k&=15,\\
a_{t,j}&=\mathbf 1[j\in\mathcal I_t].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The temperature anneals from $1.0$ to $0.5$. At rollout and evaluation, perturbations are removed and $\mathcal I_t=\operatorname{TopK}_k(\boldsymbol\ell_t)$. Crucially, the online update reuses the exact deterministic mask $\mathbf a_t$ recorded during rollout rather than sampling a different action. With standardized trajectory advantage $A_t=(U_t^H-\bar U_r^H)/(\sigma_U+10^{-8})$, it minimizes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:router_loss}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{route}}(\psi)&=
-\frac{1}{|\mathcal B_r^H|}\sum_{b_t\in\mathcal B_r^H}
A_t\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{M}
a_{t,j}\log\operatorname{softmax}(\boldsymbol\ell_t)_j\\
&\quad-\lambda_H\mathcal H(\operatorname{softmax}(\boldsymbol\ell_t)).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_H=0.01$. This computable surrogate changes router parameters, never the ordered factor manifest.

\subsection{On-Policy Distillation}
\label{sec:opd}

At round $r$, the Qwen3.5-9B student $\pi_{\theta_r}$ generates the final JSON completion and action in $\mathcal B_r$ from decision-time information; teacher supervision is therefore evaluated on current-policy prefixes rather than a fixed corpus~\cite{gkd2024,topd2026}. After horizon $H$ closes, the teacher receives exactly five scalars: utility, cumulative return, maximum drawdown, CVaR loss, and turnover. It never receives a future chart, raw price path, or OHLCV array. Let $h_t=(o_t,d_t^{\mathrm{spec}},F_t,E_t,\{m_t^i\}_{i=1}^{4})$ denote the serialized time-safe prompt and let $y_{t,<n}$ be the student-generated completion prefix. We define three full-vocabulary distributions on that same completion:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:teacher_student}
\begin{aligned}
p_{t,n}^{\theta}(\cdot)
&=\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid h_t,y_{t,<n}),\\
q_{t,n}^{\phi}(\cdot)
&=q_{\phi}(\cdot\mid h_t,y_{t,<n},
\widehat{\mathbf h}_t^H),\\
p_{t,n}^{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot)
&=\pi_{\theta_r}(\cdot\mid h_t,y_{t,<n}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Only the final policy uses trainable rank-16 LoRA over Qwen3.5-9B; the evidence specialists are structured modules. The training-only teacher is a separate frozen Qwen3.5-27B checkpoint~\cite{qwen35_2026}. Both checkpoints expose the same tokenizer vocabulary. The reference is the round-start 9B adapter snapshot, not the teacher. Deployment evaluates only $p^\theta$ and omits both the 27B teacher and $\widehat{\mathbf h}_t^H$.

\paragraph{Post-horizon agent credit.}
For net daily returns $r_{t:t+H}^{\mathrm{net}}$, turnover $\mathrm{TO}_t^H$, maximum drawdown $\mathrm{MDD}_t^H$, and positive loss magnitude $\mathrm{CVaR}_{.05,t}^H$, routing, memory admission, and coalition replay share one utility:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:utility}
\begin{aligned}
U_t^H={}&R_t^H-0.5\,\mathrm{MDD}_t^H
-0.5\,\mathrm{CVaR}_{.05,t}^H-0.1\,\mathrm{TO}_t^H,\\
R_t^H={}&\prod_{s=t}^{t+H}(1+r_s^{\mathrm{net}})-1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
All terms use decimal units; $r^{\mathrm{net}}$ already includes the stated costs, slippage, and execution delay. Credit covers the four specialists plus the final DecisionPM policy ($A=5$). For coalition $S$, exactly the roles in $S$ are enabled; other specialists emit neutral typed records, and disabling DecisionPM yields a zero-exposure hold. The same dates, market path, memory snapshot, and deterministic decoder are replayed:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:agent_credit}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf w_t(S)&=\operatorname{Replay}_H(S;o_t,F_t,E_t),\\
V_t(S)&=U^H(\mathbf w_t(S)),\\
\widehat\phi_{t,i}
&=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}
\left[V_t(P_i^{(b)}\cup\{i\})-V_t(P_i^{(b)})\right],\\
\bar\phi_{t,i}&=\widehat\phi_{t,i}/
\max\!\left(10^{-12},\sum_{j=1}^{A}|\widehat\phi_{t,j}|\right),\\
\omega_{t,i}&=
\frac{\max(0.1,\bar\phi_{t,i})}
{\sum_{j=1}^{A}\max(0.1,\bar\phi_{t,j})},
\qquad B=8.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here, $P_i^{(b)}$ precedes $i$ in sampled permutation $b$; when every raw contribution is zero we set all $\bar\phi_{t,i}=0$. Raw signed contributions are retained for audit, and the positive weights sum to one. Because only DecisionPM emits trainable tokens, $\omega_{t,\mathrm{DecisionPM}}$ scales the final-completion distillation loss. Specialist credits diagnose the evidence chain but do not pretend that the structured modules produced language tokens. Replay never changes the executed action.

\paragraph{Token objective.}
For each assistant token, both discrepancies are full-vocabulary divergences:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:opd_token}
\begin{aligned}
\bar p_{t,n}^{\theta}
&=\beta q_{t,n}^{\phi}
+(1-\beta)p_{t,n}^{\theta},\\
j_{t,n}^{\theta}
&=\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}(
q_{t,n}^{\phi}\Vert\bar p_{t,n}^{\theta})
+(1-\beta)D_{\mathrm{KL}}(
p_{t,n}^{\theta}\Vert\bar p_{t,n}^{\theta}),\\
k_{t,n}^{\theta}
&=\min\!\left\{
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_{t,n}^{\phi}\Vert p_{t,n}^{\theta}),c
\right\},
\qquad
\ell_{t,n}^{\mathrm{OPD}}=j_{t,n}^{\theta}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{tok}}k_{t,n}^{\theta}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
We use $\beta=0.5$, $c=5.0$, and $\lambda_{\mathrm{tok}}=0.1$. The KL is summed over the vocabulary before its non-negative scalar is clipped. Let $\mathcal J_r=\{(t,n):b_t\in\mathcal B_r^H,1\leq n\leq|y_t|\}$ index the assistant completion tokens. The implementation objective is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:opd_loss}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{OPD}}(\theta)={}&
\frac{1}{|\mathcal J_r|}
\sum_{(t,n)\in\mathcal J_r}
\omega_{t,\mathrm{DecisionPM}}\ell_{t,n}^{\mathrm{OPD}}\\
&+\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}\mathcal K_{\mathrm{ref}},
\quad \lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}=0.1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
JSD provides bounded two-sided alignment, while clipped $q^\phi\Vert p^\theta$ retains directional teacher pressure. Outcomes enter only through the frozen teacher and post-horizon credit.

\paragraph{Frozen-reference guard.}
The token cap controls a local teacher--student mismatch; a separate round-level guard controls cumulative drift of the language policy. For a candidate update $\theta'$ evaluated on the same prefixes, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reference_kl}
\mathcal K_{\mathrm{ref}}(\theta';\theta_r)=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal J_r|}
\sum_{(t,n)\in\mathcal J_r}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{t,n}^{\mathrm{ref}}\Vert
p_{t,n}^{\theta'}).
\end{equation}
We accept the candidate only if $\mathcal K_{\mathrm{ref}}(\theta';\theta_r)\leq\epsilon=0.1$; otherwise student and router revert to their round-start states. The guard directly constrains the student distribution; the router participates in the same transactional accept-or-revert step rather than being interpreted as KL-bounded. The teacher supplies the target, whereas the reference supplies the deployment anchor.

\subsection{Time-Safe Episodic Memory}
\label{sec:belief}

Parametric updates can average away rare, high-value situations, so we retain compact finalized episodes. For each decision, the deterministic state signature $\mathbf s_t$ concatenates up to 60 close-to-close returns and 60 within-window volume $z$-scores, then pads and $\ell_2$-normalizes to 768 dimensions. An episode pools these signatures over its rollout and stores
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:episode_schema}
e_j=\bigl(\mathbf s_j,F_j,a_j,U_j^H,
\text{asset}_j,t_j^{\mathrm{dec}},t_j^{\mathrm{avail}}\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $F_j$ is the union of routed factor IDs and $a_j$ is the dominant action/confidence pattern. Let $\mathcal U_r$ contain only utilities available by the close of round $r$. We admit episodes only after the 20-day horizon has matured and $U_j^H\geq Q_{0.8}(\mathcal U_r)$. At $C_{\max}=10{,}000$, we evict the lowest-utility entry.

Availability filtering precedes similarity search. The eligible set and retrieved top-five episodes are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:memory_score}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_t&=\{e_j\in\mathcal M_r:t_j^{\mathrm{avail}}\leq t\},\\
E_t&=\underset{e_j\in\mathcal E_t}{\operatorname{arg\,top}\text{-}5}
\;\cos(\mathbf s_t,\mathbf s_j).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The FAISS index~\cite{johnson2019billion} thus searches only outcome-matured episodes. Retrieved metadata is serialized into the 9B decision prompt as an attributed prior, never as a label.

\subsection{Constrained Policy Output}
\label{sec:policy_output}

The 9B policy receives a canonical JSON snapshot containing date, asset, current price, 1/5-day returns, 20-day volatility, current volume, the specialist proposal and rationale, the typed reasoning trace, and up to five eligible episodes. It must return one JSON object. The parser implements the deployable contract
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:selective_execution}
\begin{aligned}
(\delta_t,c_t,p_t)&\in
\{\textsc{Buy},\textsc{Sell},\textsc{Hold}\}\\
&\quad\times[0,1]\times[0,0.30],\\
p_t&=0\quad\text{if }\delta_t=\textsc{Hold}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The remaining fields are conviction, rationale, key evidence, and acknowledged risks. Deterministic decoding is used. Invalid action labels, malformed JSON, or out-of-range values trigger clipping or the deterministic specialist fallback. We make no claim of an additional learned RASW layer, regime-specific abstention thresholds, adaptive turnover rule, or trend-break guard in this implementation.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:finopd} separates point-in-time rollout from post-horizon annotation, optimization, and memory admission.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\textsc{FinOPD}: One Self-Evolution Round}
\label{alg:finopd}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE $\pi_{\theta_r},q_{\phi},R_{\psi_r},\mathcal F^\star,\mathcal M_r,H,\mathcal T_r,t_r^{\mathrm{close}}$
\STATE $\pi^{\mathrm{ref}}\gets\operatorname{Freeze}(\pi_{\theta_r})$;
$\mathcal B_r\gets\varnothing$
\STATE \textsc{Phase I: Point-in-time rollout}
\FOR{$t\in\mathcal T_r$}
\STATE $(\mathbf x_t,\mathbf s_t,\rho_t)\gets\operatorname{State}(o_t,\mathcal F^\star)$
\STATE $E_t\gets\operatorname{Retrieve}(\mathcal M_r,t,\mathbf s_t)$
\STATE $(F_t,\mathbf a_t)\gets\operatorname{Route}(R_{\psi_r},\mathcal F^\star,\mathbf x_t,\rho_t)$
\STATE $(\{m_t^i\}_{i=1}^{4},d_t^{\mathrm{spec}})
\gets\operatorname{Specialists}(o_t,F_t,E_t)$
\STATE $(y_t,\mathbf w_t)\gets\pi_{\theta_r}(o_t,d_t^{\mathrm{spec}},\{m_t^i\},F_t,E_t)$;
$\operatorname{Execute}(\mathbf w_t)$
\STATE $\mathcal B_r\gets\mathcal B_r\cup
\{(t,o_t,\mathbf x_t,\mathbf s_t,F_t,\mathbf a_t,E_t,\{m_t^i\},y_t,\mathbf w_t)\}$
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textsc{Phase II: Post-horizon update}
\STATE $\mathcal B_r^H\gets\{b_t\in\mathcal B_r:t+H\leq t_r^{\mathrm{close}}\}$
\FOR{$b_t\in\mathcal B_r^H$}
\STATE $\boldsymbol\xi_t^H\gets\operatorname{Evaluate}_H(\mathbf w_t)$;
$\widehat{\mathbf h}_t^H\gets\operatorname{Summarize}(\boldsymbol\xi_t^H)$
\STATE $\{q_{t,n}^{\phi}\}\gets q_{\phi}(b_t,\widehat{\mathbf h}_t^H)$;
$\boldsymbol\omega_t\gets\operatorname{CoalitionCredit}(b_t,\boldsymbol\xi_t^H)$
\ENDFOR
\STATE $\theta'\gets\operatorname{Update}(\theta_r,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{OPD}})$;
$\psi'\gets\operatorname{Update}(\psi_r,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{route}})$
\IF{$\mathcal K_{\mathrm{ref}}(\theta';\theta_r)\leq\epsilon$}
\STATE $(\theta_{r+1},\psi_{r+1})\gets(\theta',\psi')$
\STATE $\mathcal C_r\gets\{\operatorname{Episode}(b_t):
b_t\in\mathcal B_r^H,U_t^H\geq Q_{0.8}(\mathcal U_r)\}$
\STATE $\mathcal M_{r+1}\gets\operatorname{Cap}(\mathcal M_r\cup\mathcal C_r,C_{\max})$
\ELSE
\STATE $(\theta_{r+1},\psi_{r+1})\gets(\theta_r,\psi_r)$
\STATE $\mathcal M_{r+1}\gets\mathcal M_r$
\ENDIF
\RETURN $\theta_{r+1},\psi_{r+1},\mathcal M_{r+1}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
